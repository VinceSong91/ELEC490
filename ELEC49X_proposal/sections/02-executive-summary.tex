\UnnumberedSection{Executive Summary}

Walking golfers can already buy electric push carts that follow them around the course, but these carts only carry the bag. To know the distance to the green, or to keep track of their clubs, golfers still need a watch, a phone, or a separate tracker. The Autonomous Smart Caddy Cart (ASCC) puts both jobs on one cart, so the golfer can leave the phone in the bag.

The cart follows the golfer at a set distance, stops for obstacles, and can be stopped at any time with a remote emergency button. A reader in the bag knows which clubs are in their slots, so the cart can warn the golfer when one has been left behind. A display screen shows the distance to the green using GPS. None of this needs any input from the golfer during play. The project is deliberately limited to these three features. Extras such as shot tracking and club recommendations will only be attempted once they work.

The prototype will be judged against measurable targets. The main ones are holding a following distance of about 1.5 m, stopping within 200 ms of detecting an obstacle, and showing the distance to the green to within a few metres. The bag and the drive system will be tested separately before the whole cart is tested outdoors. The biggest technical risks are reading the club tags reliably inside the bag and losing track of the golfer. To manage them, the tag reader is tested early and the cart stops whenever it loses the golfer.

The deliverable is one working prototype, built on a used motorized golf cart and demonstrated on dry grass with one golfer and one bag of clubs. The cart should be driving under motor control by the end of October 2026, tracking clubs by December, and fully integrated and tested by March 2027. The estimated cost is 595 CAD, which fits the 600 CAD budget because the team already owns the Raspberry Pi and some of the sensors.
