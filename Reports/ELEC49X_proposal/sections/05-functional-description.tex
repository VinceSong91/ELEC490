The system architecture is organized into functional, physical, and logical domains to validate core autonomous mobility, safety, and equipment auditing without unnecessary complexity. Figure~\ref{fig:functional-architecture} outlines the primary operational blocks, showing energy and signal paths between tracking, obstacle detection, club monitoring, and motor drive systems. Figure~\ref{fig:physical-layout} defines the physical component distribution and interface boundaries across the chassis, emphasizing sensor clearance and immediate access to safety overrides (\textbf{IF-04}). Finally, Figure~\ref{fig:logical-architecture} shows how the software is layered between the hardware and the display, including the hardware communication protocols (\acs{UART}, \acs{SPI}, \acs{I2C}, and \acs{GPIO}), so that the cart stops safely when the emergency button is pressed (\textbf{PF-05}).

\CourseSubsection{Risks and safety hazards}
\Guidance{Identify relevant technical risks, health risks, and / or safety hazards, and indicate how you will manage these risks, and / or explain how system features and operating conditions are low-risk.}

The main hazard of a self-driving cart is loss of control, where the cart moves outside its safe motion limits. Figure~\ref{fig:safety-hazards} in Appendix~\ref{app:risks} maps the causes of this event, the preventative controls that make it less likely, and the protective controls that limit its consequences. Table~\ref{tab:risks} summarizes the remaining safety hazards and the main project risks, and Table~\ref{tab:risks-full} in the same appendix gives the consequence and proposed mitigation for each.
